Cross-team visibility = a share/permission on a workbook, not a copy. Deal Team owns Atlas LBO; Modeling gets read + comment; IC gets read at sign-off checkpoints. One workbook, many viewers — that's what makes "everyone sees the same history" true.

B. Where does the canonical history live? (the real product question)

Everyone can only "see each other's sheets and full history" if there's one place that holds the commits. Three ways to provide that place:

Ship first

SaaS cloud tenant

Your server is the source of truth. Teams commit into it; it holds every version, diff, author, sign-off.

  • Simplest to build & reason about
  • Cross-team history "just works" — one DB
  • SSO/SAML for org sign-on
  • Objection: "our models can't leave our walls"
→ Build this as the core
Adapter, not rival

SharePoint sync

Daemon watches the team's existing SharePoint/OneDrive synced folder; auto-ingests each saved version as a commit.

  • Meets teams where files already are
  • No behavior change → easiest adoption
  • You sit on top of SharePoint, don't replace it
  • SharePoint stays file store; you own the history/diff layer
→ Build second, as ingestion source
Enterprise ask

Self-hosted / hybrid

Same server, deployed inside their VPC. Files never leave. SharePoint as backing store, your engine on their box.

  • Answers the compliance objection
  • Kamal + their infra — your comfort zone
  • Heavier sales & support
  • Only when a real customer demands it
→ Defer until a deal requires it

Key mental model: SharePoint is a filing cabinet; you are the version-control brain. "Merging SaaS with a company's SharePoint" = the daemon treats their SharePoint folder as one ingestion source feeding your canonical history — not two systems fighting to own the file.

C. How a version actually flows in

1 · EditAnalyst edits model in Excel, saves normally
→
2 · CaptureDesktop daemon sees the save (watched folder) or user hits "Commit version"
→
3 · Parse & diffEngine aligns rows, classifies authored vs. computed, builds cascade
→
4 · StoreCommit + blobs + diff land in canonical history (cloud or self-host)
→
5 · ReviewAny permitted team opens desktop app, sees diff, comments, signs off

Steps 2 & 5 are the desktop app + daemon. Steps 3 & 4 are the engine + server. None of this requires hooking into Excel's UI — the watched-folder save is the integration point. The in-Excel colored overlay is a later, optional layer on top of this same pipeline.
